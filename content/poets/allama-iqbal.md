# Allama Muhammad Iqbal (1877-1938)

Allama Muhammad Iqbal, born on November 9, 1877, in Sialkot (now in Pakistan), is widely regarded as the greatest Urdu poet of the 20th century. Known as "Shair-e-Mashriq" (Poet of the East), Iqbal's poetry transcends time and continues to inspire millions.

## Early Life and Education

Iqbal received his early education in Sialkot. He later studied at Government College, Lahore, where he was influenced by Sir Thomas Arnold. He pursued higher studies in Europe, earning degrees from Cambridge University and the University of Munich.

## Literary Contributions

Iqbal wrote poetry in both Urdu and Persian. His major works include:

- **Bang-e-Dra** (The Call of the Marching Bell) - 1924
- **Bal-e-Jibril** (Gabriel's Wing) - 1935
- **Zarb-e-Kalim** (The Rod of Moses) - 1936
- **Asrar-e-Khudi** (Secrets of the Self) - 1915
- **Payam-e-Mashriq** (Message from the East) - 1923

## Philosophy of Khudi

Iqbal's philosophy centers on the concept of "Khudi" (Selfhood or Ego). He believed in the development of the self through continuous action, love, and faith. His famous verse "Khudi ko kar buland itna" encourages self-realization and spiritual elevation.

## Political Vision

Iqbal was also a political philosopher who envisioned a separate homeland for Muslims in the Indian subcontinent. His Presidential Address at Allahabad in 1930 is considered a foundational moment for the Pakistan Movement.

## Legacy

Iqbal passed away on April 21, 1938, in Lahore. He is honored as the national poet of Pakistan, and November 9 is celebrated as Iqbal Day. His mausoleum in Lahore is a national monument.
