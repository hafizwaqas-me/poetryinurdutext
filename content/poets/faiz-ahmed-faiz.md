# Faiz Ahmed Faiz (1911-1984)

Faiz Ahmed Faiz was one of the most celebrated poets of the Urdu language in the 20th century. Born on February 13, 1911, in Sialkot (now in Pakistan), he became known for his revolutionary poetry that combined romantic imagery with social and political themes.

## Early Life and Education

Faiz studied Arabic, English, and Philosophy at Government College, Lahore, and Murray College, Sialkot. He was influenced by the Progressive Writers' Movement and became one of its leading voices.

## Career and Activism

Faiz worked as a journalist, editor, and academic. He was editor of The Pakistan Times and served as a Major in the British Indian Army during World War II. His leftist political views led to his imprisonment during the Rawalpindi Conspiracy Case in 1951.

## Literary Contributions

Faiz wrote extensively in Urdu and some Persian. His notable works include:

- **Naqsh-e-Faryadi** (The Image of Complaint) - 1941
- **Dast-e-Saba** (Hand of the Wind) - 1952
- **Zindan-Nama** (Prison Notebook) - 1956
- **Dast-e-Tah-e-Sang** (Hand Under the Rock) - 1965

## Style and Themes

Faiz's poetry uniquely blends:

- **Romantic and revolutionary themes**: Love for beloved intertwined with love for humanity
- **Classical imagery with modern concerns**: Traditional ghazal elements with progressive ideas
- **Accessible language**: Unlike many classical poets, his poetry is approachable

## Famous Verses

His poem "Hum Dekhenge" became an anthem for resistance movements, and "Mujh Se Pehli Si Mohabbat" was immortalized in song by singer Noor Jehan.

## Recognition

Faiz was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature. He received the Lenin Peace Prize in 1962 and numerous awards in Pakistan and abroad.

## Legacy

Faiz died on November 20, 1984, in Lahore. His poetry continues to inspire artists, activists, and poets worldwide. He is remembered as a voice for the oppressed and a master of Urdu poetry.
